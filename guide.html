The Beach & Wildlife

Compass Blue is the only true beachfront vacation rental in the Kaanapali area of Lahaina — you step directly from the resort to sand. No walking through hotel corridors, no pool crossings. The beach in front of Lahaina Shores is one of the quieter stretches of this coastline.

🦭 Hawaiian Monk Seals: Critically endangered (fewer than 1,300 left on Earth), monk seals have rested on the beach directly in front of Compass Blue. If you encounter one, stay at least 50 feet away — approaching is a federal violation. It's a genuinely rare and moving experience when it happens.

Humpback Whales (November through May)

Maui's waters are the primary winter breeding and calving grounds for North Pacific humpback whales. During season (November through May), it's common to see breaches, tail slaps, and blows from shore — sometimes from the lanai. The whales are visiting Hawaiian waters; treat their presence with respectful distance and enjoy the show.

Green Sea Turtles (Year-Round)

Hawaiian green sea turtles (honu) visit the beach regularly throughout the year. Like monk seals, they are federally protected — watch from a respectful distance and let them come and go on their own terms. A turtle napping on the sand is a good turtle. Leave it be.

Diving & Snorkeling

Multiple dive and snorkel operators run out of Lahaina Harbor. The nearby waters offer excellent visibility and marine life.

From shore is free. Humpbacks are often visible just by standing on the beach or sitting on the lanai — no boat required during peak season.

Water Activities & Surf Lessons

Two surf schools, including Bully's, operate on the beach right next to Lahaina Shores — lessons and board rentals without going anywhere. The beach has good conditions for beginners.

The Lahaina Harbor area (2–3 blocks) connects you to nearly everything else: whale watching, dive boats, fishing charters, stand-up paddleboard rentals, and snorkeling excursions.

Snorkeling safety: The water in front is shallow for a long way out, which is great for beginners. Use a well-fitted mask and fins, because fins provide most of your propulsion. Skip full-face masks.

Support Local: ʻUlu o Lele Marketplace

Walkable from Compass Blue

ʻUlu o Lele Marketplace is walkable from Compass Blue and brings local retailers and restaurants together. Spend a morning there, and skip the big-box souvenir stops: your dollars go directly to the small businesses that kept Lahaina going.

Many long-time Lahaina shops have also reopened at the Cannery / Keawe Hub area, just a couple of blocks from ʻUlu o Lele. Browse in person and buy from the people who make and sell the goods.

Parking & Getting Around

The Whalers Village Parking Trick

Whalers Village in Kaanapali (about 5–6 miles away) has a parking garage, and a lost ticket costs $50. The local workaround: eat at Cool Cat Cafe, inside Whalers Village, and get their parking validation. Great burgers, and it saves you the worry.

Many guests find it easier to simply Uber to Whalers Village and back. No parking stress, no ticket, and you can have a drink while you're there.

Parking at Lahaina Shores

Resort parking is $15 per day. A free alternative: street parking is available on Shaw Street — just read the posted signs carefully and make sure you're compliant before walking away.

Getting Around the Island

A rental car is recommended for exploring beyond the Lahaina/Kaanapali area — Haleakalā, Road to Hana, and upcountry Maui all benefit from having your own wheels. For within Lahaina and Kaanapali, Uber/Lyft and walking cover most situations.

Cat tip: The resort cats you'll see throughout Kaanapali are fed and cared for by resort staff. Most are friendly. Pet them, enjoy the moment, wash your hands.

Walking Distance: Lahaina Highlights

Compass Blue's location — closest resort to historic Lahaina town — means these are a short walk:

🌳

Lahaina Banyan Tree

2–3 blocks away. Planted in 1873, it spans nearly an acre and survived the August 2023 fires — now a global symbol of Lahaina's resilience. Worth seeing at any time of day.

⚓

Lahaina Harbor

2–3 blocks. Hub for whale watching, dive boats, fishing charters, and activity tours. Also the historic heart of Lahaina's seafaring past.

🌊

The Oceanfront Walk

The new oceanfront sidewalks along Front Street are a destination on their own: open ocean, waves, rocks and wildlife the whole way. Not much else, and that's the point.

A note on Front Street: The historic Front Street shops were devastated in 2023 and the area is still rebuilding. If you walk through the surrounding neighborhoods, please keep your phone tucked away. The community appreciates respectful visitors. Many long-time businesses have reopened elsewhere, including the Cannery / Keawe Hub area.
