A bathroom with doors

Many Lahaina Shores studios were opened up so the toilet and shower share a room with the sink. Ours kept its walls and solid doors. One person can shower while another uses the vanity, which matters when friends or family share a studio.

What turns up on the beach

The sand in front of Lahaina Shores is one of the quieter stretches of this coast. These are wild animals on their own schedule, so treat every sighting as luck.

Humpback whales

November through May, with the most activity from January through March. Guests watch them breach from the beach. Whale watching boats leave from Lahaina Harbor, 2 to 3 blocks away.

Hawaiian monk seals

Endangered, with about 1,600 left according to NOAA. They have hauled out to rest on the beach in front of the building. Stay at least 50 feet back and let them sleep.

Green sea turtles

Honu are in the water year-round and sometimes nap on the sand. They are protected. Watch from a distance and let them come and go.

Surf lessons are next door: two surf schools, including Bully's, teach and rent boards on the beach beside the building. The water in front stays shallow a long way out, which suits beginner snorkelers.

Where you are

475 Front Street is the south end of Lahaina's Front Street, in the Kaanapali area of West Maui. You get a beachfront building and a kitchen without the big-resort bill, and the Kaanapali resorts are a short drive up the road.

  • Next doorSurf lessons and board rentals on the beach
  • 2 to 3 blocksLahaina Harbor: whale watching, snorkel trips and sunset sails you can walk to. Check the departure point when you book; some tours leave from Mala or Māʻalaea instead.
  • 2 to 3 blocksThe Lahaina Banyan Tree, planted in 1873, which survived the 2023 fire

  • 8 to 9 milesKapalua and the Dragon's Teeth trail

Lahaina today. Lahaina Shores, the beach, the harbor and the restaurants above are open. Historic Front Street north of the harbor is still rebuilding after the August 2023 fire, and many long-time shops have reopened at ʻUlu o Lele and the Cannery and Keawe Hub area. Shopping there is a direct way to support Lahaina.
