Wildlife on Your Doorstep

Because Compass Blue is directly on the beach — not separated from the ocean by hotel grounds — guests have a front-row experience that no resort corridor can match.

🦭

Hawaiian Monk Seals

Among the most endangered marine mammals on Earth — fewer than 1,300 remain worldwide. Hawaiian monk seals have been known to rest on the beach in front of Compass Blue. An extraordinary privilege, not a guaranteed show. NOAA asks all visitors to keep 50+ feet of respectful distance.

🐋

Humpback Whales

Each year from November through May, North Pacific humpback whales return to the warm waters of Maui to calve and breach. Watch from our beach — no boat tour required. Lahaina Harbor is also a short walk away for those who want to get out on the water foe a closer encounter.

🐢

Green Sea Turtles

Hawaiian green sea turtles (honu) are frequent visitors to the waters and beaches of Kaanapali, especially here. Year-round sightings and frequent beach rests are part of daily life at Compass Blue. The honu is a symbol of good fortune in Hawaiian culture.

Sightings are a natural gift, not a guarantee. Hawaii law requires that all marine wildlife be observed from a respectful distance.

Lahaina's Banyan Tree — A Symbol of Resilience

Planted in 1873 and spanning nearly an entire city block, the Lahaina Banyan Tree at Banyan Court Park is one of the most iconic trees in the United States. When the devastating wildfires swept through Lahaina in August 2023, the Banyan Tree survived — and became a worldwide symbol of Lahaina's enduring spirit. Compass Blue guests can walk there in minutes. Many come specifically to see it and to be part of Lahaina's story of renewal.
